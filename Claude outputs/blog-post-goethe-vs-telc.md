Short answer: it depends on who's asking for the certificate, not which exam is "better"

This is one of the most common questions we get from students starting their German journey, and the honest answer is that neither exam is objectively harder or more respected than the other. Both Goethe-Institut and telc (The European Language Certificates) are legitimate, internationally recognized German language certification bodies, and both are accepted for the vast majority of visa, university, and job applications from Pakistan. The real question isn't "which one is better" — it's "which one does the specific authority I'm applying to actually ask for." That detail matters, so always check the exact requirement on your university's admission page, your employer's job posting, or your visa checklist before booking either exam, since requirements can be specific and do change.

What Goethe-Institut is

Goethe-Institut is Germany's official cultural and language institution, so its exams carry the direct backing of the German government abroad. It offers exams across all levels, A1 through C2, and is often the default choice for university applications, since many German universities explicitly list "Goethe-Zertifikat" as an accepted proof of language ability. Exams are held at Goethe-Institut centers or their officially licensed exam partners.

What telc is

telc is a separate, equally recognized certification body based in Germany, widely used across Europe. It's especially common for two groups of learners: people applying for a family reunification visa (Familiennachzug) to join a spouse in Germany, where telc's A1 exam is frequently the one specifically named in the visa checklist, and healthcare professionals, where the telc B2 Medizin / Pflege exams are the standard requirement for doctors and nurses seeking recognition to work in Germany (something we've covered in detail in our telc B2 Medizin guide on this blog). telc exams are also often more widely available through partner language schools, which can mean shorter waiting times than a Goethe center.

So which one should you actually pick?

If you're applying to a German university, check the specific program's language requirement page first — many explicitly ask for Goethe, though some accept telc or other recognized certificates (TestDaF, ÖSD) as equivalents.

If you're applying for a spouse/family visa, telc A1 is very commonly the exact certificate named by German embassies and consulates for this route — but confirm on the embassy's current checklist, since this has been a strict, specific requirement historically and embassies are not flexible about substituting a different provider here.

If you're a doctor, nurse, or other healthcare professional seeking work authorization, telc B2 Medizin (or telc B1/B2 Pflege for nursing) is the standard most German state medical boards ask for.

If you're applying for a job outside healthcare or for an Ausbildung (vocational training) position, either certificate is usually accepted — check the specific employer's posting, since this varies.

If none of the above applies to you yet and you just want a recognized general certificate, Goethe is the safer default simply because it's the most universally recognized name.

The one thing not to do

Don't pick an exam based on which one "sounds easier" or based on rumors about pass rates — both are administered to the same CEFR (Common European Framework) standard at each level, so a B1 from either provider represents the same real ability. What actually decides your choice is the specific requirement of the institution you're applying to. If you're not sure which one you need, bring the exact requirement text to your instructor before you register — it's a five-minute conversation that can save you from booking (and paying for) the wrong exam.

We offer preparation tracks for both Goethe and telc exams across A1-B2, so once you know which certificate your visa, university, or employer needs, we can build your class plan around that specific exam format.
